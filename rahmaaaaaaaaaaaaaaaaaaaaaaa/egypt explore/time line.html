Unified Egypt (3100 BC)

The unification of Upper and Lower Egypt under the first pharaoh, marking the beginning of Egyptian dynastic history and the birth of one of the world's greatest civilizations.

Old Kingdom (2686–2181 BC)

Known as the "Age of the Pyramids," this era featured monumental architecture like the Great Pyramids of Giza and a powerful centralized state ruled by divine kings.

Middle Kingdom (2055–1650 BC)

A period of reunification and cultural flourishing after political fragmentation. Famous for advances in literature, art, and more refined architecture.

New Kingdom (1550–1070 BC)

The most prosperous and powerful period of ancient Egypt. The empire expanded greatly and magnificent temples such as Karnak and Abu Simbel were built.

Ptolemaic Egypt (332–30 BC)

Founded by Ptolemy I after Alexander the Great. This era blended Greek and Egyptian cultures and ended with the famous reign of Cleopatra VII.

Roman Egypt (30 BC–395 AD)

Egypt became a vital province of the Roman Empire, known as the empire's grain supplier, while continuing its unique cultural and religious traditions.
