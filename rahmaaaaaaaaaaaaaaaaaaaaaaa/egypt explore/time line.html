Unified Egypt (3100 BC)

The unification of Upper and Lower Egypt under the first pharaoh, marking the beginning of Egyptian dynastic history.

Old Kingdom (2686–2181 BC)

Known as the "Age of the Pyramids," this era featured monumental architecture like the Giza Pyramids and a strong centralized state.

Middle Kingdom (2055–1650 BC)

A period of reunification following political fragmentation, renowned for advances in arts, literature, and architectural refinement.

New Kingdom (1550–1070 BC)

The New Kingdom was the most prosperous period of ancient Egypt. Egypt expanded its territory and built magnificent temples.

Ptolemaic Egypt (332–30 BC)

Begun by Alexander the Great's general Ptolemy, this era blended Greek and Egyptian cultures, ending with Cleopatra VII.

Roman Egypt (30 BC–395 AD)

Egypt became a vital grain-supplying province of the Roman Empire, transitioning culturally through Roman and later Byzantine rules.
