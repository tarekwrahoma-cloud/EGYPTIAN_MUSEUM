MILITARY HISTORY

WARS THAT SHAPED EGYPT

☀

From the first unification of the Two Lands to the last great stand of the New Kingdom — six campaigns that built, defended, and redrew the borders of ancient Egypt.

1
c. 3100 BC

Unification of Egypt

𓋹 King Narmer (Menes)

The campaign that created Egypt itself. Narmer, ruler of Upper Egypt, conquered the northern Delta kingdoms and merged Upper and Lower Egypt into a single state — the founding act of the Egyptian civilization and its first dynasty.

2
c. 1550 BC

The War to Expel the Hyksos

𓋹 Ahmose I

After nearly a century of Hyksos rule over the Delta, the liberation war begun by Seqenenre Tao and carried on by Kamose was completed by Ahmose I, who besieged their capital Avaris and drove them out of Egypt for good, founding the New Kingdom.

3
c. 1457 BC

The Battle of Megiddo

𓋹 Thutmose III

Facing a Levantine coalition at Megiddo, Thutmose III gambled on a narrow mountain pass to catch his enemies off guard. The surprise attack broke the rebellion and earned him a reputation as one of history's most brilliant military strategists.

4
c. 1274 BC

The Battle of Kadesh

𓋹 Ramesses II

One of the largest chariot battles ever fought. Ramesses II was nearly trapped by the Hittites but rallied his army to survive the ambush — a clash that later produced history's oldest known peace treaty.

5
c. 1208 BC

Merneptah's Libyan War

𓋹 Merneptah

When Libyan forces and allied raiders pushed into the western Delta, Merneptah met and crushed them in a decisive battle. He commemorated the victory on a stone stela that holds the earliest known reference to Israel.

6
c. 1178 BC

War Against the Sea Peoples

𓋹 Ramesses III

As the Sea Peoples toppled empires across the Mediterranean, Ramesses III lured their fleet into the Nile Delta and destroyed it with a coordinated ambush of ships and shore archers — saving Egypt from the collapse that ended the Bronze Age.
